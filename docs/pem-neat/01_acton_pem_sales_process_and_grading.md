# Acton ADU Partnership Evaluation Meeting (PEM) Sales Process & Grading Standard

## Purpose

This document defines the Acton ADU sales process used during a Partnership Evaluation Meeting (PEM) and the standard an AI sales analyst should use to judge how well an advisor executed the meeting.

Acton's process is a company-specific hybrid based primarily on Sandler principles. The goal of a PEM is not simply to present Acton or sell an ADU. The advisor must determine whether there is a mutually appropriate partnership by understanding the prospect's motivations, pain, budget, decision process, schedule, project constraints, and expectations of a construction partner.

The advisor should maintain control of the process while remaining conversational. Natural wording is expected. Grade the function and substance of each step, not memorization of a script.

---

# 1. Core Philosophy

A strong PEM should:

1. Build trust.
2. Establish a clear agreement about how the meeting will work.
3. Understand why the homeowner is considering an ADU.
4. Find the deeper pain behind that reason.
5. Understand why choosing the right builder matters.
6. Determine whether budget and financing are realistic.
7. Understand who decides, how they decide, and when.
8. Establish schedule goals.
9. Summarize the prospect's situation and confirm understanding.
10. Determine whether Acton is actually a good fit.
11. If qualified, connect Acton's process directly to the prospect's stated needs and risks.
12. Secure a clear next step.
13. Reinforce the commitment through a post-sell conversation.

The advisor is not required to force a sale. A respectful disqualification or recommendation of another path can represent excellent sales execution.

---

# 2. Evidence Standard

Analyze only what the transcript supports.

## Tier 1: Explicit evidence

Treat direct statements as facts.

Examples:

- "We need this for my parents."
- "Our budget is around $400,000."
- "My husband and I will decide."
- "We want to choose someone within three weeks."

Quote important evidence when useful.

## Tier 2: Linguistic indicators

Behavior in the text may be described as an indicator, but not as emotional fact.

Examples:

- Prospect begins asking detailed questions about next steps.
- Prospect uses forward-looking language such as "when we build."
- Prospect volunteers additional project details without prompting.

Describe these as indicators, not certainty.

## Tier 3: Unsupported emotional inference

Do not infer tone, excitement, discomfort, hesitation, frustration, confidence, or similar states unless explicitly expressed in the transcript.

If the transcript cannot establish something, say so.

---

# 3. Speaker Attribution

If speakers are labeled, preserve those labels.

If the transcript is unlabeled, infer REP vs. PROSPECT using:

- question direction
- company/process knowledge
- personal/property information
- pronouns
- agenda control
- question/answer adjacency
- names/direct address

Do not build important conclusions on ambiguous attribution.

If attribution is materially uncertain, state the limitation.

---

# 4. The Acton PEM Process

## Step 1: Bonding & Rapport

### Objective

Establish trust and create a comfortable professional conversation.

The advisor should adapt to the homeowner's communication style without losing control of the meeting.

The advisor should also explain their role. Substance should resemble:

> My role is to understand the project requirements and your needs so I can make an honest assessment and recommendation about how best to serve you.

Exact wording is not required.

### Look for

- natural connection
- appropriate conversational warmth
- active listening
- acknowledgment of customer statements
- credibility without excessive pitching
- explanation of the advisor's role
- evidence that trust develops throughout the meeting

### Common misses

- excessive small talk
- rapport that consumes meeting time without advancing trust
- jumping into product/features immediately
- failing to explain the advisor's role
- dominating the conversation

---

# 5. Step 2: PALO / Up-Front Contract

PALO establishes the meeting contract.

## P — Purpose

The advisor should clearly explain why the meeting is happening.

Successful substance:

- understand the homeowner's project and needs
- evaluate mutual fit
- make an honest recommendation

### Grade questions

- Was the purpose explicit?
- Was it concise and understandable?
- Did the advisor position themselves as an evaluator/advisor rather than merely a presenter?

---

## A — Agenda

The advisor should establish a verbal roadmap and obtain reasonable buy-in.

The agenda should substantially cover:

1. Prospect agenda items: ask what the homeowner wants to make sure is covered.
2. Motivation/use case: why build the ADU?
3. Concerns and partner criteria: what could go wrong and what matters in a builder?
4. Budget: permission to discuss financial goals openly.
5. Decision process: who participates and how the partner/design decision will be made?
6. Schedule: desired timing and move-in/completion goals.
7. Summary: recap needs before presenting the solution.
8. Recommendation/fit: Acton may or may not be appropriate; if not, recommend another path; if yes, explain how Acton would address the project.

### Grade questions

- Did the advisor ask for the prospect's agenda first?
- Did they listen to and acknowledge those requested topics?
- Did they explain the major meeting topics?
- Did they receive explicit or practical buy-in?

Do not require exact wording or a rigid numbered recitation.

---

## L — Logistics

The advisor should establish the available meeting time.

Example substance:

> I have about 90 minutes. Do you still have that much time?

If the homeowner has a hard stop, the advisor should acknowledge it and adjust.

### Grade questions

- Was meeting duration discussed?
- Was a hard stop identified?
- If the available time changed, did the advisor recontract around the new time?

---

## O — Outcome

The advisor should define the possible result of the meeting before deep discovery.

The important concept is mutual decision-making.

Successful substance:

> At the end, we should know whether this is a partnership worth pursuing. If it is not, I can recommend another direction. If it is, we can discuss the appropriate next step.

Acton commonly frames three possible outcomes:

1. Not a fit; leave as friends and/or provide another recommendation.
2. Mutual fit; discuss the next Acton step.
3. Prospect needs defined additional time/information before deciding.

### Grade questions

- Was the outcome explicit?
- Was mutual fit emphasized?
- Did the prospect agree?
- Was "think it over" treated as a defined outcome rather than an uncontrolled ending?

PALO should occur before substantial discovery whenever practical.

---

# 6. Step 3: Pain Discovery

Pain is split into Type 1 and Type 2.

The advisor should find Type 1 first, then Type 2.

---

## Type 1 Pain: Why Build an ADU?

### Objective

Understand the functional reason and the deeper practical/emotional problem that makes the project matter.

A use case is not automatically pain.

Example:

Reason:
> We want an ADU for Mom.

Pain:
> Mom is becoming vulnerable living alone, the family is worried about her safety, and they need a way to keep her close without eliminating everyone's independence.

Common Type 1 themes include:

- aging parents
- multigenerational living
- boomerang children
- caretaker housing
- rental income
- downsizing
- future aging in place
- lack of space
- privacy
- entertainment
- cramped main house
- safety
- isolation
- broken promises or long-delayed family goals
- investment/property utilization
- lifestyle or workspace needs

### Pain Funnel behavior

When a reason appears, the advisor should dig deeper.

Useful functions include:

- Tell me more about that.
- How long has that been an issue?
- What happens today because you do not have the space?
- How often does that happen?
- How does that affect the family?
- What happens if you do nothing?
- Why is this becoming important now?
- What would solving it change?
- What alternatives have you considered?

Do not grade based on exact questions. Grade whether the advisor moved from surface reason to meaningful consequence.

### Strong Type 1 discovery establishes

- why an ADU
- why now
- current consequences
- future consequences
- alternatives
- importance/priority
- what success would change

### Common miss

The advisor hears "office," "parents," or "rental" and immediately starts presenting floor plans.

---

## Type 2 Pain: Why the Right Partner / Why Acton?

### Objective

Understand the homeowner's concerns about executing the project and what they need from a builder.

Typical Type 2 pain:

- poor prior contractor experience
- contractor disappearing or poor communication
- vague proposals
- unclear scope
- cost overruns
- change orders
- fear of hidden site costs
- permitting complexity
- utility complexity
- project management burden
- schedule uncertainty
- quality concerns
- poor closeout/punch experience
- lack of accountability
- fragmented architect/GC process
- desire for predictable pricing
- desire for professional guidance
- need for transparency
- need for risk management before committing to a large project

### Strong Type 2 discovery establishes

- what the homeowner fears
- prior construction experiences
- what they liked/disliked previously
- what "good" looks like this time
- what criteria will differentiate builders
- whether they value Acton's design-build/risk-management model
- whether they are primarily seeking the lowest bid

### Important distinction

Do not manufacture "Why Acton" after the fact. Type 2 pain must come from the prospect's needs and concerns. Acton's solution should later be mapped to those needs.

---

# 7. Step 4: Budget

## Objective

Determine what the prospect is both able and willing to invest, what their number includes, and how the project will be funded.

The advisor should treat budget directly and professionally.

### Explore

- stated range
- target
- comfortable number
- hard ceiling if available
- all-in vs. structure-only
- site work assumptions
- design/engineering
- permits/fees
- upgrades/finishes
- landscaping or other exclusions
- contingency expectations
- how the budget was created
- quotes from competitors
- ROI assumptions
- financing source

Potential financing:

- cash
- HELOC
- construction loan
- ADU lender
- other loan
- mixed funding

### Key distinction

Ability to spend is not the same as willingness to spend.

If someone can access $500k but only believes the project is worth $350k, the working budget is not automatically $500k.

### Strong budget discovery

The advisor tests whether the project remains viable if realistic costs differ from the prospect's initial assumption.

### Common misses

- accepting the first number without follow-up
- failing to clarify "all-in"
- quoting price before understanding budget
- discussing financing only after it becomes a problem
- treating competitor estimates as equivalent without checking scope

---

# 8. Step 5: Decision-Making Process

## Objective

Understand who decides, what they will compare, what information they need, and when a decision can occur.

Explore:

- all financial decision makers
- design decision makers
- spouse/partner
- family members
- board/funder/trustee/advisor if relevant
- absent decision makers
- competing builders/options
- comparison criteria
- approval process
- decision timeline
- information required before a yes/no
- whether the next meeting should include another stakeholder

### Strong discovery answers

- Who?
- How?
- Based on what?
- Against which alternatives?
- By when?

### Common misses

- identifying a spouse but not their role
- learning competitors exist but not how they will be compared
- leaving "we need to think" undefined
- not establishing a decision date or decision window

---

# 9. Step 6: Schedule

## Objective

Establish the customer's desired project timeline.

Explore:

- desired completion/move-in
- desired construction start
- decision date
- permitting expectations
- outside events driving timing
- whether timing is urgent, flexible, or exploratory

The advisor should distinguish the customer's desired schedule from Acton's estimated process timeline.

---

# 10. Step 7: Summary

## Objective

Before fulfillment/presentation, the advisor should stop and accurately recap the prospect's situation.

The recap should substantially include:

- Type 1 pain
- Type 2 pain
- budget
- decision process
- schedule
- important project requirements/constraints

Then obtain confirmation that the summary is accurate.

The advisor should reinforce the meeting outcome: based on what has been learned, decide whether Acton appears to be a fit before presenting.

### Strong summary

Sounds like the homeowner's story, not a generic list.

### Common misses

- no recap
- recap only the desired floor plan
- omit pain or budget
- present Acton before confirming understanding

---

# 11. Step 8: Fulfillment

## Objective

Determine qualification first. Present second.

### Qualification

A qualified opportunity generally requires sufficient alignment in:

- pain/motivation
- budget/financial path
- decision process
- schedule
- project feasibility/fit

If a material issue exists, the advisor should address it directly.

If the project is not a fit, the advisor should respectfully disqualify and, where useful, recommend another approach.

### Solution positioning

If qualified, the advisor should connect Acton's process specifically to the prospect's Type 1 and Type 2 pain.

Do not reward a generic company presentation as highly as a tailored one.

Relevant Acton concepts may include:

- ADU-specialist design-build model
- coordinated design, permitting, estimating, project management, and construction
- Feasibility Package as an early risk-management step
- investigation of property/site/utility constraints before major commitment
- transparent scope and pricing
- Build Ready plans where appropriate
- custom design where appropriate
- professional project management
- construction communication systems
- milestone/process visibility
- reducing avoidable surprises/change orders through upfront planning

### Substance over wording

The rep does not need a memorized value proposition. Score whether the customer benefit and causal logic were communicated.

Example:

Weak:
> We use Buildertrend.

Stronger:
> You said you hated not knowing what was happening on your last project. We use Buildertrend so schedule, selections, updates, and project communication are visible in one place.

The second is tailored to Type 2 pain.

---

# 12. Step 9: Outcome / Close

## Objective

End with a clear, mutually understood next step.

Possible outcomes:

- YES: prospect agrees to proceed with the next Acton step.
- NO: prospect/Acton decides not to proceed.
- DECISION DATE: prospect needs time or information and agrees to a specific decision/follow-up timeline.

A next step should ideally contain:

- action
- owner
- date/time or defined timing
- required inputs
- expected result

Examples:

- schedule Feasibility Package site inspection
- send agreement/payment link
- spouse joins recap meeting
- prospect speaks to lender
- Acton sends plan set
- follow-up meeting on a defined date

"Think about it and let me know" is weak.

---

# 13. Step 10: Post-Sell

## Objective

After apparent agreement, briefly revisit a meaningful concern to test whether the commitment is durable.

The advisor can surface a concern that previously appeared and verify it will not become a later stumbling block.

Example function:

> Before we wrap, earlier you said the biggest concern was the total site-work number. Are you comfortable moving into feasibility knowing that its job is to clarify that before you make the larger commitment?

### Strong post-sell

- revisits a real concern
- does not create artificial fear
- confirms commitment
- exposes hidden objections if present

### Common miss

Ending immediately after a yes without checking whether unresolved issues remain.

---

# 14. Structural Control

Beyond whether topics were mentioned, judge how well the advisor ran the PEM as a structured event.

A strong call:

- establishes PALO early
- follows discovery before fulfillment
- explores Type 1 before Type 2
- handles budget and decision process directly
- summarizes before presenting
- presents only relevant solutions
- closes to a defined next step
- avoids unnecessary feature dumps and tangents

Do not over-penalize natural conversational detours if the advisor deliberately returns to the process.

---

# 15. Scoring Standard

Score each category 1–10.

## 9–10: Excellent

Complete, deliberate, customer-specific execution. Strong evidence of the intended sales function and little meaningful improvement needed.

## 7–8: Strong

Core objective achieved, with one or two clear opportunities to deepen, tighten, or control the step.

## 5–6: Partial

Important parts were present, but execution was shallow, incomplete, out of sequence, or insufficiently confirmed.

## 3–4: Weak

The topic appeared, but the intended sales function was mostly missed.

## 1–2: Missing / materially ineffective

Little or no evidence of the step, or execution actively damaged the process.

Do not mechanically average scores unless explicitly requested. Provide an overall judgment separately.

---

# 16. Required Sales Assessment Scorecard

Evaluate:

1. Bonding & Rapport
2. PALO / Up-Front Contract
   - Purpose
   - Agenda
   - Logistics
   - Outcome
3. Type 1 Pain
4. Type 2 Pain
5. Budget
6. Decision-Making Process
7. Schedule
8. Summary
9. Fulfillment / Solution Positioning
10. Outcome / Close
11. Post-Sell
12. Overall Process Control

For each:

- score 1–10
- status: COMPLETED / PARTIAL / MISSED / N-A where useful
- evidence
- what worked
- specific coaching opportunity

---

# 17. Coaching Rules

Coaching must be behavioral and tied to evidence.

Bad:
> Dig deeper into pain.

Good:
> When the homeowner said the kids take over the living room every weekend, the advisor confirmed it was important but moved on. Ask one consequence question such as, "What does that make weekends like for you and your husband?" before changing topics.

Identify:

- top strengths
- top improvement areas
- the single behavior that would most improve the next PEM

Do not flatter the rep. Do not manufacture criticism when execution was strong.

---

# 18. Quantitative Conversation Metrics

When speaker attribution is reliable, optionally calculate:

- rep vs. prospect word ratio
- rep open-ended discovery question count
- question classification: discovery / qualifying / closed-procedural
- longest uninterrupted rep monologue in words
- prospect questions
- forward-looking language
- unsolicited detail

Flag rep talk share above roughly 60%, but context matters: fulfillment naturally increases rep talk time.

Metrics support the assessment; they do not replace process judgment.

---

# 19. Transcript Quality

If the transcript is truncated, corrupted, missing the ending, or has uncertain attribution:

- state the limitation
- do not score missing material as definitively missed when the evidence may simply be absent
- use "Not determinable from provided transcript" when appropriate
- lower confidence in affected categories

Never invent missing events, commitments, prices, or quotes.
