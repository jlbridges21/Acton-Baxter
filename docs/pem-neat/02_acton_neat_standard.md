# Acton ADU NEAT Standard

## Definition

NEAT is the internal sales intelligence artifact produced from a Partnership Evaluation Meeting (PEM) transcript.

NEAT stands for:

- **N — Notes**
- **E — Email**
- **A — Assessment**
- **T — Transcript**

A NEAT has two jobs:

1. Give the sales team a fast, accurate understanding of the opportunity and the prospect's buying situation.
2. Evaluate how well the advisor executed Acton's Sandler-based PEM process so the sales manager can coach the advisor.

The source of truth is the meeting transcript. The analyst may organize, infer cautiously, and synthesize, but must not invent facts.

---

# 1. Audience

The NEAT is an internal sales document.

The sales manager should be able to read the first portion and quickly understand:

- the customer's story
- Type 1 pain
- Type 2 pain
- budget
- decision process
- timing
- competition
- fit
- next step
- sales execution quality

Project-specific information may later be copied into operational systems if the prospect signs, so operational notes must remain factual and clean.

---

# 2. N — NOTES

Notes are not a chronological transcript summary. They are structured sales intelligence.

## Customer Story

Write a concise 3–5 sentence narrative explaining:

- homeowner/family/property context
- why an ADU is being considered
- why now
- long-term goal
- how the ADU fits the customer's life plan

Prioritize the human story over technical details.

---

## Customer Pain

Summarize the central problem the customer is trying to solve.

Explain what is not working today, what consequence exists, and why action matters.

Distinguish symptoms from the deeper driver.

---

## Type 1 Pain — Why Build an ADU?

Provide 1–3 concise bullets.

Capture the functional need plus deeper consequence where supported.

Examples:

- Bring aging parents close to family while preserving independence and reducing concern about their current living situation.
- Create dedicated work/family space because the main home is already maxed out and daily activities compete for the same rooms.
- Generate rental income from an underutilized property as part of a long-term investment strategy.

---

## Type 2 Pain — Why the Right Builder / Why Acton?

Capture the prospect's builder-selection pain.

Examples:

- wants transparent scope after receiving vague proposals
- fears surprise site-work/change-order costs
- does not want to manage architect, contractor, and trades separately again
- needs a responsive partner after being ghosted
- values quality and accountability over lowest bid
- wants risk investigated before making a large commitment

Do not turn Acton features into customer pain. Start with what the customer said they need or want to avoid.

---

## Budget

Capture all known financial intelligence:

- stated range
- target/hard ceiling if known
- whether all-in
- what is included/excluded
- competitor price anchors
- financing source
- lender status
- cash/HELOC/construction loan/ADU lender
- whether exploratory or firm
- unresolved budget risk

If the prospect says "$400k" but the transcript shows that number excludes site work, say so explicitly.

---

## Decision-Making Process

Capture:

- all decision makers
- absent stakeholders
- design decision makers
- financial approvers
- competitors/options
- decision criteria
- decision timeline/date
- outside influences
- information still required

---

## Schedule

Capture separately:

- desired decision date
- desired start
- desired completion/move-in
- schedule drivers
- flexibility/urgency
- external dependencies

---

## Competition / Alternatives

When relevant, note:

- other builders
- prefab/modular
- architect + bid approach
- remodel/addition/garage conversion
- moving/renting
- doing nothing
- what the prospect likes/dislikes about alternatives

---

## Acton Recommendation

In 3–5 sentences explain whether Acton appears to be a good fit and why.

Tie the recommendation to the customer's actual needs.

Common fit reasons:

- design-build coordination
- Feasibility Package/risk management
- upfront site/utility investigation
- transparent pricing/scope
- Build Ready plan fit
- custom design capability
- full-service project management
- communication/process visibility

If fit is weak, say so.

---

## Next Steps

Use actionable bullets.

For each next step, capture:

- action
- responsible party
- timing/date if known

Separate homeowner and Acton actions when useful.

---

## Meeting Outcome

Use one:

### YES

The prospect explicitly agreed to move forward with the defined next Acton step.

### NO

The prospect or Acton decided not to pursue the opportunity.

### DECISION DATE

The prospect has not committed but a defined decision/follow-up date or window exists.

If none is established, use **DECISION DATE — NOT SECURED** and explain that the rep left without a firm decision timeline.

Do not call general interest a YES.

---

# 3. E — EMAIL

The Email is the customer-facing follow-up draft.

It should:

- thank the prospect
- prove the advisor listened
- restate the customer's goals in their language
- summarize the most important requirements/concerns
- state the recommended next step
- list concrete actions
- avoid internal sales language such as Type 1 pain, Type 2 pain, qualification, score, close, or sales assessment

Tone:

- warm
- professional
- concise
- specific
- not generic
- no pressure beyond what was established in the meeting

Do not introduce promises, pricing, dates, scope, or technical conclusions that were not supported by the meeting.

If a decision maker was absent and a recap was discussed, include that as a next step.

---

# 4. A — ASSESSMENT

The Assessment is internal coaching for the sales manager.

It evaluates the advisor against the Acton PEM process, not whether the prospect happened to like the advisor.

Use the separate Acton PEM Sales Process & Grading Standard as the governing rubric.

## Required assessment categories

1. Bonding & Rapport
2. PALO / Up-Front Contract
3. Type 1 Pain
4. Type 2 Pain
5. Budget
6. Decision-Making Process
7. Schedule
8. Summary
9. Fulfillment / Acton Solution Positioning
10. Outcome / Close
11. Post-Sell
12. Overall Process Control

For each:

- score 1–10
- evidence
- what worked
- what should improve

When timestamps exist, cite them.

Quote selectively. Do not fill the assessment with transcript excerpts.

## Overall assessment

Conclude with:

- overall grade or overall score if requested by the workflow
- top 3 strengths
- top 3 improvement areas
- "The One Thing": the single behavior with the highest coaching leverage

The assessment should be candid, specific, and useful for coaching.

---

# 5. T — TRANSCRIPT

Preserve the source transcript as the evidence record.

Do not silently rewrite the transcript to make the rep sound better.

Permitted cleanup:

- obvious formatting
- speaker labels when attribution is sufficiently reliable
- paragraph breaks
- timestamps
- removal of duplicate transcription artifacts only when clearly duplicate

Do not alter substantive wording.

If the transcript is too long for the destination system, preserve the full transcript separately and link/reference it according to the implementation environment.

---

# 6. Project Intelligence

A NEAT may also include a structured Project Intelligence section for information that could later support design, estimating, or production.

Only include operationally useful facts.

Examples:

- desired ADU size
- bedrooms/bathrooms
- layout requirements
- accessibility
- build location
- lot constraints
- setbacks/easements
- trees
- demolition
- access
- electrical panel
- sewer
- water
- gas
- trenching
- utility concerns
- grading/slope
- existing structures
- survey status
- foundation preferences
- exterior/style requirements

Never convert speculation into a confirmed site condition.

Use labels such as:

- confirmed
- homeowner-reported
- advisor estimate
- unknown / needs feasibility verification

when ambiguity matters.

---

# 7. Production Notes

Use concise bullets for high-value construction considerations.

Examples:

- Existing panel capacity unknown; verify during feasibility.
- Long sewer route appears likely based on homeowner description.
- Protected tree near proposed build area.
- Existing deck/pergola may require demolition.
- Homeowner prefers zero-threshold shower for aging parents.
- Boundary survey status unknown.

Production notes are not a place for sales commentary.

---

# 8. Internal Opportunity Notes

Maximum 2,500 characters.

Summarize:

- opportunity strength
- customer intent
- Type 1/Type 2 drivers
- budget alignment
- decision process
- competition
- risks
- follow-up priority
- current outcome

This should function as a CRM-quality internal snapshot.

---

# 9. What a Good NEAT Is Not

A NEAT is not:

- a transcript summary with every topic mentioned
- a generic CRM note
- a customer proposal
- a sales pitch
- a fabricated psychological profile
- a feature list
- a scorecard without evidence

A strong NEAT tells the sales manager:

> Why does this customer care, can they buy, how will they decide, why might Acton win or lose, what happens next, and how well did the advisor run the process?

---

# 10. Fidelity Rules

1. Transcript is the source of truth.
2. Never invent quotes.
3. Never invent customer commitments.
4. Never convert an advisor's estimate into a confirmed price.
5. Never convert a prospect's assumption into a verified site fact.
6. Separate customer statements from advisor claims.
7. Distinguish stated budget from realistic Acton cost if both appear.
8. Distinguish desired schedule from promised schedule.
9. If information is missing, write "Not established."
10. If the transcript is incomplete, say which conclusions are uncertain.
