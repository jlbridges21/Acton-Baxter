## 2. The Three Pillars

The core statement rests on three ideas. Sales tends to lead with the first (certainty); marketing tends to lead with the outcome of the second and third (quality and long-term performance). They're two halves of one story, and the strongest conversations use both.

## Pillar 1 — Certainty throughout the process

We reduce surprises instead of creating them. We spend real time understanding a property before we make recommendations, because we've learned the site often drives cost as much as the ADU itself. We understand what a property requires, translate that into dollars and cents, and only then figure out what's left for design. The payoff for the customer is a project agreement we can confidently stand behind—and a reputation for not sending change orders unless the customer changes the scope.
Customer translation: “I'll know what I'm actually getting, and the number won't move on me.”

## Pillar 2 — Quality in the finished product

Two 500-square-foot ADUs are not built equal—and they're not worth the same, either. It's no different than two 1,500-square-foot homes, where one sells for $800K and another for $1.4M. Same square footage; the quality, design, and craftsmanship are the difference. We start by asking how a customer will actually live in the space, then design around that use case. Better design and quality don't just feel better—they perform better.
Customer translation: “This is a real home I'll be proud of, not a box in the backyard.”

## Pillar 3 — A home built to perform for decades

We're not designing for how someone will use the space this year—we're designing for five, twelve, thirty years out. Maybe today it's for an aging parent; down the road it's for adult children; after that it's a rental or a place to downsize into. That optionality is exactly what makes an ADU valuable, and it's why quality is an investment rather than a cost.
Customer translation: “Whatever life throws at me, this space adapts—and it holds its value.”

## 3. The Financial Story Behind Quality

When a customer's real question is “will this pay off?”, quality is the financial answer. This is the framing from the webinar—use it when the conversation turns to ROI.
A lot of homeowners initially focus on one number: “I need this ADU to make me $500 or $800 a month.” That's understandable, but our savviest customers evaluate the investment in three buckets:

### 1. Day-one equity

A well-designed, high-quality, traditionally built ADU is often valued at a similar price per square foot as the main home. Appraisal studies on our completed projects consistently show homeowners adding more value to their property than they invested. Invest $500K and it isn't uncommon for the property to be worth $600K or $700K more once complete. That's not cash tomorrow—but it's real equity you own and can borrow against.

### 2. Wealth creation

Once that value is added, the ADU appreciates along with the rest of the home. Fast-forward ten or fifteen years and that can mean hundreds of thousands of dollars in additional wealth.

### 3. Monthly cash flow

We think of this as the icing—not the cake. Rent helps pay the bills or offset the mortgage, but investors who truly understand real estate aren't putting in hundreds of thousands just to make a few hundred a month. They're building long-term wealth. And that's exactly why quality matters: if the goal is to maximize long-term wealth, quality isn't a luxury—it's one of the biggest drivers of return.

## 4. Applying It — The Inquiry Call

Goal of this moment: create curiosity and set expectations. The inquiry call is our shorter, information-gathering conversation. The value prop's job here isn't to close—it's to frame why we're different so the customer wants to keep talking.
When to say it: right after the prospect tells you what they're looking for in a builder—especially if price comes up. Acknowledge that they should talk to multiple companies first; it makes the pitch feel like guidance, not a sales push.

Why it works: it's not salesy. It leads with the anchor line, sets the price expectation honestly, and previews certainty and quality without drowning the customer in detail.

## 6. Applying It — Other Situations

The same core message flexes into almost any moment. Keep the anchor line intact and adjust only the framing around it.

### When a prospect pushes on price

Don't defend the number—reframe what the number buys. “We're usually not the lowest-priced option. Homeowners choose Acton for certainty and quality, not the lowest bid. We invest more upfront in understanding your property so the price we give you is one we can stand behind.”

### When a prospect asks “what do you mean by certainty?”

Now you earn the right to use the supporting detail. This is where the site-condition stories belong: “The same ADU on two different properties can price out five figures apart. Are we trenching five feet or 500? Through dirt, or through a concrete driveway? Overhead electrical, or a more expensive underground upgrade? Does the ADU require fire sprinklers, a special foundation, or special tree removal? We price what your property actually requires before we talk floor plans.”

### In a follow-up email or proposal

Lead with marketing's outcome language, close with the anchor line. “Live better, earn more, grow in value. Not all ADUs are built the same—and they're not worth the same, either. Homeowners choose Acton for certainty throughout the process, quality in the finished product, and a home that's built to perform for decades.”

### In a webinar or one-to-many setting

Use the three financial buckets—day-one equity, wealth creation, monthly cash flow—to make the case that quality is a wealth strategy, then land on the anchor line.

## 7. What to Keep In vs. Save for Later

A value proposition creates curiosity; it doesn't tell the whole story. Some of our best material is too detailed for the spoken pitch and works far better as a follow-up answer once the customer asks. Keep the pitch tight; hold these in reserve.

### Keep in the spoken value prop

The anchor line—every time, word for word.
Certainty (we understand the site before we price) and quality (we design around how you'll actually live).
“We're building a home that needs to perform for decades.”
The honest price framing: we're not the lowest, and that's on purpose.

### Save for when the customer asks

The full Acton Construction → Acton ADU history and the 2017 code change.
Specific trenching and electrical-panel examples.
The architect's award count and “over 500 homes” credentials.
The detailed $300K vs. $400K breakdown (summarize live, hand over the full numbers).
